    Renewal models¶

    The three renewal models, with the maths of each.

    The data and notation are on the Data methods page. How the models are built in code and sampled is on the Implementation page.

    Overview¶

    renewal fits the provinces together, pools them and links them by importation. renewalsp fits the province model to each province on its own, with no pooling or importation. renewalbvd is renewal with a down-weighted likelihood term on the Rt of BVDOutbreakSize. seabbs-renewal submits the first of renewalbvd, renewalsp, renewal, randomwalknowcast and randomwalk, in that order, to pass its checks (Submission workflow). The sections below give the model of each province, then what renewal adds to pool and link the provinces, then how renewalsp and renewalbvd differ. Each gives only the maths it adds. Every time-varying part is a weekly random walk or AR(1), so it stays near constant unless the data show change.

    Each province \(p\) the release lists has the model below.

    Time axis¶

    The data weeks start one week before weekly onsets, summed over the modelled provinces, first reach 5, or their peak if lower. The data weeks end with the week that holds \(T\), the release's cut-off (Data). The model axis starts 42 days (6 weeks) earlier, the length of the generation interval, for seeding. Every weekly walk is held at its starting value over the 6 seeding weeks and the first data week. The fit axis is the data weeks, extended for renewalbvd as below.

    Reproduction number¶

    \[ \log R_p(t) = \log R_p(t - 1) + \sigma_R z(t), \qquad \sigma_R \sim \mathcal{N}^+(0, 0.2^2), \]

    with standard normal steps \(z(t)\), sampled non-centred, and a start \(\mathcal{N}(0, 0.5^2)\).

    Renewal¶

    The renewal runs on infections per resident, dated by infection:

    \[ x_p(t) = R_p(t) \sum_{j \ge 1} x_p(t - j)\, g(j). \]

    \(g\) is BVDOutbreakSize's Gamma generation interval (mean 15.3, SD 9.3 days), double interval censored as \(G(d + 1) - 2G(d) + G(d - 1)\) with \(G\) the integrated cdf, on lags 1 to 42 and renormalised (mean 14.8, SD 8.4 days), as in primarycensored12. Seeds are \(e_p \sim \text{LogNormal}(0, 1.5^2)\) infections per day that will be confirmed, or \(I_{0,p} = e_p / (\alpha_0 N_p)\) infections per resident, on the first data day. The seed is projected back over the seeding weeks at the growth rate implied by \(R_p\) and \(g\), clipped to \([-0.05, 0.3]\) per day.

    Ascertainment¶

    Onsets follow infections after the incubation period \(\eta\), and expected confirmed onsets are

    \[ \lambda_p(s) = \alpha(s)\, N_p \sum_{u = 0}^{28} x_p(s - u)\, \eta(u), \qquad \operatorname{logit} \alpha(s) = \operatorname{logit} \alpha_0 + a(s), \]

    with \(N_p\) the province population, \(\alpha_0 = 0.6\) fixed and \(a\) a weekly random walk from zero with step SD \(\mathcal{N}^+(0, 0.03^2)\). \(\eta\) is a log-normal incubation period for Bundibugyo virus disease. Its mean \(\mu_\eta \sim \mathcal{N}(6.3, 0.54^2)\) and SD \(\sigma_\eta \sim \mathcal{N}(3.5, 0.8^2)\), both truncated below at 1 day, have BVDOutbreakSize's priors. The mean prior reproduces the 95% CI of 5.2 to 7.3 days from the 2007 Uganda outbreak3. MacNeil et al. give no interval on the spread, so the SD prior is BVDOutbreakSize's weakly informative choice around 3.5 days. Each draw of \(\eta\) is double interval censored in the same way on lags 0 to 28, the 99.9% quantile at the prior means.

    Delays¶

    The onset-to-report delay \(f_s(d)\), on days \(d\) from 0 to 60, comes from a discrete hazard that varies with onset day:

    \[ \operatorname{logit} H_s(d) = \beta(d) + \gamma(s), \qquad f_s(d) = H_s(d) \prod_{j < d}\{1 - H_s(j)\}, \qquad H_s(60) = 1. \]
    • \(\beta\) is a random walk over delay, \(\beta(0) \sim \mathcal{N}(-2, 1)\) with step SD \(\mathcal{N}^+(0, 0.4^2)\), so the shape is smooth but not parametric.
    • \(\gamma\) is a weekly random walk over onset day from zero, with step SD \(\mathcal{N}^+(0, 0.15^2)\), shifting all hazards together.

    Missing onsets¶

    Cases reported on day \(t\) without an onset are a share \(m_p(t)\) of reports, with \(\operatorname{logit} m_p(t) = \bar m(t)\). \(\bar m\) is a weekly random walk with start \(\mathcal{N}(-2, 1)\) and step SD \(\mathcal{N}^+(0, 0.3^2)\).

    Known onsets¶

    A case reported on day \(t\) is in the vintage with probability \(\Pi(t) = F_\ell(T - t)\), with \(T\) the vintage's cut-off and \(F_\ell\) the cdf of the report-to-publication lag. The lag is the gap between a case's report and its first appearance in a release. Its pmf on 0 to 7 days has a Dirichlet(1, ..., 1) prior and is learnt from consecutive releases (see Likelihood). Expected known onsets, right truncated by vintage, are

    \[ \mathrm{known}_{p,T}(s) = \lambda_p(s) \sum_{d} f_s(d)\, \{1 - m_p(s + d)\}\, \Pi(s + d). \]

    Hub imputation¶

    The hub gives each case reported without an onset one by subtracting a delay drawn from a fit to its published delays (imputation details). We model that procedure, bias included:

    \[ M_p(t) = m_p(t) \sum_s \lambda_p(s)\, f_s(t - s), \qquad \mathrm{imputed}_{p,T}(s) = \sum_{t = s}^{T} \hat f_T(t - s)\, \Pi(t)\, M_p(t). \]

    \(M_p(t)\) are the cases reported on day \(t\) without an onset. The hub draws a report hour from \(\mathrm{Unif}(0, 24)\) and subtracts a generalised gamma delay in hours fitted to the non-negative delays published at 2026-10-17. \(\hat f_T\) is the daily pmf this implies, \(\hat f_T(k) = \int_0^1 \{F_T(k + u) - F_T(k - 1 + u)\}\, du\) with \(F_T\) the delay cdf in days, on 0 to 60 days, and is data. It is the double interval censored pmf, computed in closed form from the generalised gamma's partial first moment. The hub fits the delays as exact for releases up to 2026-10-24 and as double interval censored from 2026-11-01 (hub issue 75), and \(F_T\) is the fit for release \(T\).

    Likelihood¶

    • Weekly \(\mathrm{known}_{p,T}\) and \(\mathrm{imputed}_{p,T}\) are negative binomial, each stream with size \(\phi\), \(1/\sqrt{\phi} \sim \text{HalfNormal}(0.25)\) and \(\phi\) kept below 1000.
    • Known-onset delays, by province, onset week and delay on 0 to 60 days, are Dirichlet-multinomial given each province and week's total. Their probabilities come from \(\lambda\), \(f\), \(m\) and \(\Pi\), with concentration \(\psi\), \(1/\sqrt{\psi} \sim \text{HalfNormal}(0.1)\) and \(\psi\) kept below \(10^4\). Delays below 0 count as 0 and delays above 60 as 60.
    • Each delay-file row (location, onset week and delay) of release \(k + 1\) was already in release \(k\) with probability $\(q = (1 - b_k) \frac{\sum_u F_\ell(T_k - t_u)}{\sum_u F_\ell(T_{k+1} - t_u)},\)$ averaged over the onset days \(u\) of the row's week, with \(t_u\) the report day for onset \(u\) and \(T_k\) release \(k\)'s cut-off. \(b_k \sim \text{Beta}(1, 19)\) is the share of rows that appear late whatever their age, such as onsets recorded later for cases first counted as imputed. The kept rows of each cell are beta-binomial with mean \(q\) and concentration \(1/(\tau^2 + 10^{-3})\), \(\tau \sim \mathcal{N}^+(0, 0.5^2)\), kept below 1000 as the other concentrations are. Rows up to 60 days old at release \(k\) enter. This term conditions on the later release, so it informs \(F_\ell\) only.

    Forecasts¶

    The random walks carry \(\alpha\) and \(f\) to the forecast horizon, \(m\) keeps its last value, and each of 100 trajectories is one posterior draw. Past the fit axis the walk of \(\log R\) reverts towards 0 with a fixed half-life of 2 weeks, \(\log R(t) = 2^{-1/2} \log R(t - 1) + \sigma_R z(t)\), so it does not compound growth over the horizon. In renewal this is the pooled trend, and the province deviations keep their AR(1) (below). The reversion leaves the fit unchanged. A trajectory is the final known plus final imputed counts. Final known counts are those reported by the cut-off plus a negative binomial draw of those reported later, as in epinowcast. Final imputed counts are a negative binomial draw centred on the current imputed count plus those expected later, since the hub re-imputes each vintage. The national trajectory is the sum of the provinces plus an unassigned component. The component is \(\max\{0, Y_{CD}(w) - \sum_p Y_p(w)\}\) in data weeks and \(\rho \sum_p \tilde y_{p,i}(w)\) after, with \(\tilde y_{p,i}\) trajectory \(i\) of province \(p\). \(\rho\) is the component's share of the provinces' total over the last 4 data weeks.

    Partially pooled (renewal)¶

    renewal fits every province the release lists in one model, on the axis set by their summed onsets. The provinces share the trend \(\log R(t)\) and the deviation SD \(\sigma_\delta\), half-life \(t_{1/2}\) and start spread \(\sigma_v\). They also share ascertainment \(\alpha\), the publication lag, the trend \(\bar m\) of the missing-onset share, the share at risk \(\nu\) and the importation share \(\varepsilon\). Each seeded province has its own deviations \(\delta_p\) and \(\omega_p\) and its own seed. Provinces are linked by importation through \(W\). Each seeded province has its own delay, pooled towards the shared delay. Leaving a province out removes its known and imputed counts and its delay counts but keeps it in the latent process.

    Pooled reproduction number¶

    \[ \log R_p(t) = \log R(t) + \delta_p(t). \]

    The pooled trend \(\log R(t)\) is the unweighted mean of \(\log R_p(t)\), not the national Rt, and is the walk of the province model. The province deviations sum to zero. They are \(\delta_p(t) = \sum_j Q_{pj}\, \xi_j(t)\), with \(Q\) an orthonormal basis of the deviations that sum to zero over the \(P_s\) seeded provinces and are 0 for the rest, and \(P_s - 1\) weekly AR(1) processes \(\xi_j\) that decay towards 0, the pooled trend,

    \[ \xi_j(t) = \kappa\, \xi_j(t - 1) + \sigma_\delta z_j(t), \qquad \kappa = 2^{-1/t_{1/2}}, \qquad t_{1/2} \sim \text{LogNormal}(\log 6, 0.6^2)\ \text{weeks}, \qquad \sigma_\delta \sim \mathcal{N}^+(0, 0.1^2). \]

    The shared \(\sigma_\delta\) and \(t_{1/2}\) pool the provinces. The starting offsets are \(\xi_j(0) = v_j\), with \(v_j \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_v^2)\) and \(\sigma_v \sim \mathcal{N}^+(0, 0.3^2)\), so \(\delta_p(0) = \sum_j Q_{pj} v_j\).

    Importation and depletion¶

    The province model's renewal gains importation and a share at risk:

    \[ \Phi_p(t) = R_p(t) \sum_q W_{pq} \sum_{j \ge 1} x_q(t - j)\, g(j), \qquad x_p(t) = S_p(t)\big\{1 - e^{-\Phi_p(t)/\nu}\big\}, \qquad S_p(t + 1) = S_p(t)\, e^{-\Phi_p(t)/\nu}. \]
    • \(W = (1 - \varepsilon) I + \varepsilon\, \mathrm{diag}(1/N) K^\top \mathrm{diag}(N)\) mixes transmission between provinces, with \(\varepsilon \sim \text{Beta}(1, 100)\).
    • \(K\) is a row-normalised gravity kernel, \(K_{qp} \propto N_p e^{-D_{qp}/300\,\text{km}}\) for \(q \ne p\), with \(D\) the great-circle distance between population-weighted province centres.
    • \(\nu \sim \text{LogNormal}(\log 0.05, 0.75^2)\) is the share at risk. \(S_p\) starts at \(\nu\) less the seed, so infections never exceed \(\nu N_p\).

    Provinces with cases in the first data week are seeded at the start. Later provinces start at \(10^{-6}\) of that level and add \(I_{0,p}\) to \(\Phi_p(t)\) each day for 7 days. These 7 days start 5 days before the province's first case week.

    Province delays¶

    Each province's delay hazard adds a sum-to-zero offset \(a_p\) and a sum-to-zero weekly walk \(g_p(s)\) to the shared shift \(\gamma(s)\), on the basis \(Q\):

    \[ \operatorname{logit} h_{p,s}(d) = \beta(d) + \gamma(s) + a_p + g_p(s). \]

    The offsets have spread \(\mathcal{N}^+(0, 0.5^2)\) and the walks start at 0 with step SD \(\mathcal{N}^+(0, 0.1^2)\), both sampled non-centred. So each province's delay follows the shared one over time, with its own level and its own drift, each shrunk towards the shared delay by a learnt spread. As for \(\delta_p\), \(Q\) spans the seeded provinces, so a province never seeded has no delay data and takes the shared delay. Known-onset delays are scored by province. Health zones take their province's delay, since the delay files list provinces only.

    Missing-onset deviations¶

    The missing-onset share gains sum-to-zero province deviations \(\omega_p\) on the same basis \(Q\):

    \[ \operatorname{logit} m_p(t) = \bar m(t) + \omega_p(t). \]

    The deviations are random walks, not AR(1). They start with province spread \(\mathcal{N}^+(0, 0.5^2)\), sampled non-centred, and have step SD \(\mathcal{N}^+(0, 0.1^2)\).

    Provinces with no cases¶

    A province with no case yet starts at \(10^{-6}\) of its seed level and is never seeded. Its deviations \(\delta_p\) and \(\omega_p\) are 0, so it follows the pooled trends. Its seed is fixed at the prior median, 1, and nothing is sampled for it. Its infections come only from importation through \(W\), and its zero counts are scored like any other province's. Its forecasts follow from the model, so every province the release lists has one.

    Single patch (renewalsp)¶

    renewalsp fits the province model to each province's series on its own, one fit after another with the same code. Nothing is pooled or shared between provinces: each has its own Rt walk, delay, missing-onset share and ascertainment. One series cannot inform the share at risk, so there is no depletion. Every province with a case in the random walks' walk window is fitted, however few its cases. A province with none takes the introduction layer of randomwalknowcast (Baseline models): exactly 0 until an introduction, then its walk. National is the sum of the provinces plus the data's national gap, as for renewal. A province is fitted only on its own data, so renewalsp cannot forecast a province left out.

    BVDOutbreakSize-informed (renewalbvd)¶

    Every renewal model takes from the BVDOutbreakSize main model its generation interval, the priors of the incubation period, the prior on the half-life of the province deviations and the sum-to-zero basis for them. renewalbvd also takes the Rt estimates of one BVDOutbreakSize release, chosen as on the Data methods page. Without such a release renewalbvd is renewal.

    From the release it takes the posterior draws of the national \(\log R\) and, where the release has province estimates, each patch's median and 90% interval with the hub provinces the patch covers. Both models date Rt by infection, so the estimates are aligned to the release's data cut-off \(t_b\). The fit axis extends to the end of the week of \(t_b\) when that week is after the data. For the nation CD and each patch \(k\) with a modelled province, the model's Rt before depletion, \(R_p\), is weighted by infections over the data week that holds \(t_b\),

    \[ R_{\mathcal{G}}(t_b) = \frac{\sum_{p \in \mathcal{G}} \Omega_p\, R_p(t_b)}{\sum_{p \in \mathcal{G}} \Omega_p}, \qquad \Omega_p = N_p \sum_{t \in \text{week}(t_b)} x_p(t), \qquad \mathcal{G} \in \{\text{CD}, k\}. \]

    With the term down-weighted by \(\zeta = 0.5\), the likelihood gains

    \[ \mu_{\text{CD}} \sim \mathcal{N}\big(\log R_{\text{CD}}(t_b),\, \sigma_{\text{CD}}^2/\zeta\big), \qquad \tilde\mu_k - \tilde\mu_{\text{CD}} \sim \mathcal{N}\big(\log R_k(t_b) - \log R_{\text{CD}}(t_b),\, \sigma_{\Delta,k}^2/\zeta\big), \qquad \sigma_{\Delta,k}^2 = \max(\tilde\sigma_k^2 - \tilde\sigma_{\text{CD}}^2,\, 0.05^2). \]

    \(\mu_{\text{CD}}\) and \(\sigma_{\text{CD}}\) are the mean and SD of the release's national log Rt draws, converted from growth rates for early releases (Data). \(\tilde\mu_k\) and \(\tilde\sigma_k\) are each patch's log median and SD, the SD from the 90% interval. \(\tilde\mu_{\text{CD}}\) and \(\tilde\sigma_{\text{CD}}\) are the same summaries of the national estimate the patches are relative to, or \(\mu_{\text{CD}}\) and \(\sigma_{\text{CD}}\) if the release has none. Leaving a province out drops the national term and the patch that holds it. The term adds no parameters, and \(\zeta = 0\) gives renewal.

    Health zones¶

    Zone samples sum to their province's samples, sample by sample and week by week, and zones do not enter the national value. The random walk baselines run at zone level and their samples set the zone shares of the province samples (Baseline models). Every other model's province forecast is split across the zones.

    Split of province samples¶

    For province \(p\) with zones \(z\), let \(o_z(w)\) be zone \(z\)'s published count for week \(w\) and \(O(w) = \sum_z o_z(w)\). Each province sample \(i\) draws its zone shares once, for all weeks,

    \[ \theta^{(i)} \sim \text{Dirichlet}(\chi), \qquad \chi_z = r_z + n_0\, \frac{l_z + \chi_0}{\sum_{z'} (l_{z'} + \chi_0)}. \]

    \(r_z\) is the zone's onsets over the 2 weeks before the vintage and \(l_z\) those over the 16 weeks before it. Recent shares are shrunk towards longer-run shares by \(n_0 = 100\) pseudo-cases. The floor \(\chi_0 = 1/2\) gives a zone with no cases a small share. Province samples are rounded. Each zone keeps its published count and the rest of the province sample \(Y^{(i)}(w)\) is split,

    \[ \big(y^{(i)}_z(w)\big)_z = \big(o_z(w)\big)_z + \text{Multinomial}\big(Y^{(i)}(w) - O(w),\ \theta^{(i)}\big). \]

    A sample below the published total is drawn from the published zone counts without replacement instead.

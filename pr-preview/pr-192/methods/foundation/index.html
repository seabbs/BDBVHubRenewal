    Foundation models¶

    Six experimental candidate models that give the hub target's releases to the Chronos-2 time series foundation model1.

    The data and notation are on the Data methods page. The models are named fm_chronos_<source>_<layout>, with two sources of the hub target and two layouts. None is fitted to these data: Chronos-2 forecasts in context, from its pretrained weights or a fine-tuned checkpoint. The two fm_chronos_<source>_ragged_ft models are the ragged models with Chronos-2 fine-tuned on simulated outbreaks (Fine-tuning). Each is a candidate, not submitted. The hub model seabbs-chronos2_ft is set up to submit fm_chronos_daily_ragged_ft and is off until it is opted in (Opting in).

    In brief, each model:

    1. lays out the hub target of every release by place, step and release lag (Releases);
    2. puts the country and every province, each as its own hub-target series, in one block that Chronos-2 forecasts jointly (Layouts);
    3. draws 100 trajectories from Chronos-2's quantiles (Trajectories);
    4. rounds the draws to counts no lower than the count already reported (Counts).

    Releases¶

    The target is the hub's: confirmed cases by onset week, known and imputed onsets together. Two sources give it with its updates.

    • Weekly (fm_chronos_weekly_*): the weekly counts of each release of the target data. A step is an onset week.
    • Daily (fm_chronos_daily_*): the daily counts of each release, known plus imputed onsets. A step is an onset day.

    For place \(p\), step \(t\) and release \(r\) with cut-off in step \(c_r\), the release lag is \(d = c_r - t\). \(Y_{p,t,d}\) is the count of step \(t\) at lag \(d\), from the release whose cut-off is \(d\) steps after \(t\). Release \(r\) gives lag \(d\) only at step \(c_r - d\), so each lag has at most one value per release and is missing at other steps. \(X_{p,t}\) is the count of step \(t\) at the vintage, with cut-off step \(c\).

    Layouts¶

    The country and all 26 provinces are each a series in one block, each the hub target itself, so the country is not the sum of the provinces. Chronos-2 forecasts every target series in the block jointly, from one shared last step of context, \(t^*\), which each layout sets differently. Health zones are split from the provinces as for the other fitted models (Health zones).

    • Complete (*_complete): the targets \(X_{p,t}\) for \(t \le t^*\). \(t^*\) is the earliest over places of the week randomwalk starts its walk from, the latest week 95% complete under its delay (Baseline models). It comes from the delay alone, so it needs no walk spread.
    • Ragged (*_ragged): the same targets, with one lag series per place and lag \(d < D\), indexed by onset step as in a regression nowcast. Lag series \(d\) holds \(Y_{p,t,d}\), missing where it is not yet reported, and Chronos-2 masks the missing values. \(t^* = c - D\), the last step whose count is at lag \(D\) or more, with \(D = 4\) weeks or \(D = 28\) days. The lag series run past \(t^*\) as known future values, which is how recent counts reach the nowcast.

    Chronos-2 standardises each series and takes its arcsinh, so counts go in as they are.

    Trajectories¶

    Chronos-2 gives 21 quantiles, from 1% to 99%, of each series at each step ahead. Each of the 100 trajectories draws its own uniform level for every series and step, and reads the quantile function there: linear between the quantiles and flat beyond them. Draws are independent across steps and series.

    Counts¶

    A draw is rounded to the nearest count and floored at zero. A step's draw is no lower than its count at the vintage, and steps up to \(t^*\) are their count at the vintage. The daily source is summed to weeks, as the hub's target is weekly, and each week is no lower than its reported count.

    Patches and context¶

    Chronos-2 reads a series in patches of 16 steps. The context is every step from the first onset in the releases to \(t^*\).

    Scoring¶

    The models forecast final counts and model no delay. So the release backtest scores them on the complete target only, the weeks the latest release reports in full (Evaluation).

    Fine-tuning¶

    The _ft models load one LoRA checkpoint of Chronos-2, trained on 2,000 outbreaks simulated with epinowcast's generative model (scripts/finetune_simulate.R). The simulator, enw_simulate, is on epinowcast's feature/829-simulate-forecast branch, pinned at commit e084b080, and is not yet in a release. Each outbreak is a renewal process with a drawn log \(R_t\) path, a reporting delay that drifts and differs by place, day-of-week effects and negative binomial noise, in one to 26 places. Each gives a daily reporting triangle, and its weekly releases are summed from it. A training block cuts an outbreak's releases at one cut-off and lays them out as the ragged layout does, with the target past \(t^*\) the outbreak's final counts (forecasters.finetune.training_block). \(D\) is drawn from 2 to 7 weeks, or 14 to 49 days, so the model is not tied to one \(D\). Each outbreak gives up to four weekly and four daily blocks, with the country and a random subset of its places. A block holds at most 26 provinces when weekly and five when daily, so daily blocks fit GPU memory. Training is 1,000 steps of LoRA at learning rate \(10^{-4}\), with 5% of blocks held out (scripts/finetune_chronos.py).

    A checkpoint is named by its settings hash, of the blocks, the code and the training settings. The first release, chronos-ft-2c2b22a03107, was trained from earlier code, so its hash cannot be reproduced from this repository. Later releases are trained from the code here, so retraining from it gives the same hash. Each checkpoint is published as the repository's release chronos-ft-<hash>, with a sha256sums.txt of its files. The models load the newest such release by publish date, unless config/finetune.toml pins a hash. The checkpoint's hash and adapter SHA-256 key the stored fits, so a new release refits the _ft models, and each stored fit records the checkpoint it used. The adapter is downloaded once and checked against sha256sums.txt and the settings hash. The fit store resolves the release once per run, with the workflow's GitHub token, and passes its hash to every fit in BDBV_CHRONOS_FT. If GitHub cannot be reached, building a model fails rather than fall back to a checkpoint downloaded before. A fit whose adapter cannot be downloaded is stored as failed, and the fit store keeps it until its inputs change. Such fits are retried by running the Fit store workflow with retry_failed (gh workflow run fit-store.yml -R seabbs/BDBVHubRenewal -f retry_failed=true -f models="fm_chronos_weekly_ragged_ft fm_chronos_daily_ragged_ft").

    Limitations¶

    • The zero-shot models use Chronos-2 as pretrained. It has not seen these data or outbreaks like them.
    • The fine-tuned models have seen only simulated outbreaks, whose delays and dynamics may not match these data.
    • The daily fine-tuned model was trained on blocks of at most five provinces but forecasts all 26 in one block.
    • Draws are independent across steps and places, so every weekly value of the daily models, a sum of seven daily draws, is too narrow, as are sums over weeks or places.

Foundation model setup and fine-tuning

NotePlan
  • We describe how Chronos-2 and LoRA work, with schematics.
  • We give every setting of the model and the fine-tune.
  • Key references: (Ansari et al. 2025; Hu et al. 2021).

Attention biases. Chronos-2’s group attention has no notion of which series is which, so on its own it can link a lag series to its place’s counts only by matching their values.

  • We add four learned biases to Chronos-2’s group-attention scores (Table 1).
  • They start at zero, so fine-tuning starts from base Chronos-2, as for the relation biases TableFormer adds to a pretrained model (Yang et al. 2022).
  • The biases train at a learning rate of \(10^{-3}\) and the LoRA adapter at \(10^{-5}\), both falling linearly to zero at 8,000 outbreaks.
  • We identify places by name within each block, with no limit on the number of places.
  • A place’s parent is the nearest prefix of its name in the block, else the country.
  • Training blocks include countries equal to, above or later than the sum of their places, so a place belongs to its parent without summing to it.
  • The biases follow Moirai’s any-variate attention bias (Woo et al. 2024), and the relative position biases of T5 (Raffel et al. 2020) and the structural biases of Graphormer (Ying et al. 2021).
  • In Moirai’s ablation, additive learned variate embeddings gave a normalised MAE of 0.904 on the Monash benchmark, against 0.655 with the attention bias (Woo et al. 2024).

Group attention

  • Chronos-2 attends over time within each series, then over the series of a block at each time step, which is group attention (Ansari et al. 2025).
  • At one time step, row \(i\) of a block (a place’s count or one of its lag series) attends to row \(j\) with weight \[ a_{ij} = \operatorname{softmax}_j\!\left(q_i^\top k_j + M_{ij}\right), \] where \(q_i\) and \(k_j\) are the query and key vectors of one head in one layer, and \(M_{ij}\) is the group mask, \(0\) when rows \(i\) and \(j\) are in the same block and \(-\infty\) otherwise.
  • Chronos-2 does not divide the scores by the square root of the key dimension.

Attention biases

  • Each row \(i\) belongs to a place \(P(i)\), and each place \(p\) has a parent \(\pi(p)\) in the block, or none.
  • We add a learned bias for each relation \(r\) to the scores: \[ a_{ij} = \operatorname{softmax}_j\!\left(q_i^\top k_j + M_{ij} + \sum_{r} b_r^{(l,h)}\,\mathbb{1}\!\left[(i,j) \in R_r\right]\right), \] with one scalar \(b_r^{(l,h)}\) per relation \(r\), layer \(l\) and head \(h\), and \(b_r^{(l,h)} = 0\) at the start.
  • The relations are the pairs of rows in Table 1: \[ \begin{aligned} R_\text{self} &= \{(i, j) : i = j\},\\ R_\text{same} &= \{(i, j) : i \neq j,\ P(i) = P(j)\},\\ R_\text{up} &= \{(i, j) : P(j) = \pi(P(i))\},\\ R_\text{down} &= \{(i, j) : P(i) = \pi(P(j))\}. \end{aligned} \]
  • A pair in no relation gets no bias, as in Moirai’s any-variate attention (Woo et al. 2024) and TableFormer’s relation biases (Yang et al. 2022).

Mixed-frequency blocks

  • One fine-tuned Chronos-2 model forecasts daily, 3-day and weekly data.
  • A training block is daily, 3-day or weekly, or daily with one aggregate row per place (Table 2).
  • A place’s aggregate row holds its 3-day or weekly totals, \[ y^{(k)}_{p,t} = \begin{cases} \sum_{s=t-k+1}^{t} y_{p,s} & \text{if day } t \text{ ends a period of } k \text{ days},\\ \text{missing} & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases} \] where \(y_{p,s}\) is place \(p\)’s daily count on day \(s\) and \(k = 7\) for weekly or \(k = 3\) for 3-day totals.
  • The aggregate row has no lag series of its own.
  • It belongs to its place, so it is in that place’s same-place relation.
  • The loss counts the aggregate row only on period-end days, where \(m_{n,t} = 1\), and its missing days have \(m_{n,t} = 0\).

LoRA

  • LoRA replaces a weight matrix \(W \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\text{out} \times d_\text{in}}\) with \[ W' = W + \frac{\alpha}{r} B A, \] where \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_\text{in}}\) and \(B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\text{out} \times r}\) are trained and \(W\) is frozen (Hu et al. 2021).
  • We use rank \(r = 8\) and \(\alpha = 16\).
  • We apply it to the query, key, value and output projections of the time and group attention of all 12 layers, and to the output layer.
  • \(B\) starts at zero, so \(W' = W\) at the start.

Loss

  • Chronos-2 scales each row by the mean \(\mu_n\) and standard deviation \(\sigma_n\) of its context, then applies \(\operatorname{arcsinh}\), so a target \(y_{n,t}\) becomes \(\tilde y_{n,t} = \operatorname{arcsinh}\!\left((y_{n,t} - \mu_n)/\sigma_n\right)\).
  • The model gives a quantile \(\hat q_{n,t,k}\) at each of 21 levels \(\tau_k \in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.10, \dots, 0.95, 0.99\}\).
  • The loss is the quantile (pinball) loss, averaged over the \(H\) steps and the \(N\) rows of a batch and summed over levels: \[ L = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{1}{H} \sum_{t=1}^{H} m_{n,t} \sum_{k=1}^{21} 2 \left| \left(\tilde y_{n,t} - \hat q_{n,t,k}\right) \left(\mathbb{1}\!\left[\tilde y_{n,t} \le \hat q_{n,t,k}\right] - \tau_k\right) \right|, \] where \(m_{n,t}\) is 1 for a target step to forecast and 0 for a missing target or a lag series’ known future value.

Learning rates

  • The learning rate at step \(t\) falls linearly from \(\eta_0\) to zero: \[ \eta_t = \eta_0 \left(1 - \frac{t}{T}\right), \] with \(T = 36{,}500\) steps, which is reached at 8,000 simulations.
  • \(\eta_0 = 10^{-5}\) for the LoRA adapter, as in Chronos-2, and \(\eta_0 = 10^{-3}\) for the attention biases.

Training blocks. A simulation gives daily counts by onset and report date, and a release counts every case reported by its cut-off, one cut-off a week. Each simulation gives 10 observation draws, and at each of 16 cut-offs 2 of the 10 draws are sampled, so one simulation gives 32 blocks. Each block’s prediction length is its horizon, and the LoRA learning rate falls linearly from \(10^{-5}\) to zero at 8,000 simulations, with checkpoints at 1,000, 2,000, 4,000 and 8,000.

  • We apply further options on top of the layout, each drawn independently.
  • 10% of the blocks hold every report to one randomly chosen weekday, so reference dates stay daily and reports arrive in weekly batches, as in weekly surveillance reporting.
  • 20% of the blocks hold a single series, the country or one place.
  • The other blocks hold a random set of places, with or without the country.
  • Where a block holds the country and some of its places, a third each have a country equal to the sum of its places, above it (with cases not assigned to a place), or later than it (its own stream).
  • The options combine, so a block can be, for example, daily with a weekly row, batched and non-summing.
  • Non-summing parents apply only where a block holds both the country and its places.
  • Table 3 gives the realised share of each combination.

Order of the training data. The grid is a crossed factorial design balanced in every block of 120 simulations, with each factor’s levels shuffled independently, as in a Latin hypercube. Training takes the simulations in chunks of 250, about two balanced blocks. A chunk gives about 6,700 training blocks, which are shuffled and packed into about 1,250 steps of up to 256 series each. So every checkpoint has seen the whole grid, and no factor level comes in a run of its own.

Chronos-2 divides a flat context by a scale of \(10^{-5}\), and we apply no floor to that scale at inference. A floor of one count at inference only hurts small series, of five counts or fewer, on held-out simulations. At 4,000 outbreaks their median loss is ?meta:evidence.scale_floor.sims.n4000.small.median_loss.none without it and ?meta:evidence.scale_floor.sims.n4000.small.median_loss.floor with it, and the share of 99% quantiles over 100 times the truth is ?meta:evidence.scale_floor.sims.n4000.small.q99_over_100x.none% and ?meta:evidence.scale_floor.sims.n4000.small.q99_over_100x.floor%. Large series are unchanged, with median loss ?meta:evidence.scale_floor.sims.n4000.large.median_loss.none and ?meta:evidence.scale_floor.sims.n4000.large.median_loss.floor. On the BDBV release backtest the floor gives a lower relative WIS at every fine-tuned checkpoint, ?meta:evidence.scale_floor.bdbv.provinces.weekly.n4000.floor against ?meta:evidence.scale_floor.bdbv.provinces.weekly.n4000.none for the weekly model at 4,000 outbreaks on provinces. Chronos-2’s widest interval is from 1% to 99%, as it has no 2.5% or 97.5% quantiles.

The ragged layout’s lag series run to 4 weeks or 28 days. Lags run to the truth delay do worse with short release histories. On the German development half, the national WIS relative to randomwalknowcast is 2.91 with the 4-week cap and 3.79 with lags to the truth delay for the daily zero-shot model, and 4.44 and 8.01 for the weekly one. On the BDBV release backtest it is 2.78 and 5.84 for the daily zero-shot model, and 1.3 and 3.17 for the weekly one. BDBV’s zones have few weekly releases, so with the longer horizon no recent lag of a zone falls in the model’s context.

To compare training on simulations with training on real data, we also fine-tuned Chronos-2 on real data alone (fm_chronos_daily_ragged_real and fm_chronos_weekly_ragged_real). It was trained on the German data and the BDBV data together. The German part used the data as available on 2022-02-09, the last day of the German development half, and nothing later. German training blocks were cut at every nowcast date whose truth, four weeks ahead, was at least 40 days old by then, from 2021-04-20 to 2021-12-03. Each date was laid out as the German nowcast tasks are, with the same block functions as the simulations, from the daily and weekly releases, for Germany alone and with its states or age groups, at release lags of 14 to 49 days (including 40) or 2 to 7 weeks. The hub file’s changes after 80 days hold revisions made up to August 2022, so they were left out. The BDBV part used every public release up to 2026-11-08 (cut-off 2026-11-05). Daily blocks were cut at each daily release, and weekly blocks at each week from the hub’s public per-case delay table, for the country alone and with its provinces, at every release lag. Truth was the latest release’s counts, left out after 2026-10-01, so every count trained on was at least the BDBV truth delay of 35 days old. Training followed Chronos-2’s recipe as the simulation fine-tune does, from base Chronos-2, for its default 1,000 steps. The last 14 German nowcast dates were held out to monitor the loss.

The epinowcast model used to simulate outbreaks

We simulated each synthetic outbreak from the generative model of epinowcast (placeholder?). We used enw_simulate(), which runs the epinowcast Stan model with fixed parameters and no likelihood, and draws reported counts from its generated quantities. enw_simulate() is on the feature/829-simulate-forecast branch of epinowcast, at commit e084b080. The simulation script is scripts/finetune_simulate.R, with grid r4. Each simulation is one set of fixed parameters with several observation draws, and we evaluate one draw of each. Simulations follow a crossed design, in which every level of every factor is balanced within each block of simulations. Continuous settings are drawn within a simulation’s cell, as the SI table lists. A parameter we did not set is drawn from its epinowcast prior.

Design factors
  • Generative process (process, through enw_expectation()): growth, renewal_3, renewal_7, renewal_15.
  • Generation time shape (gt_shape, through enw_expectation(generation_time)): gamma_0.3, gamma_0.6, gamma_1, lognormal_0.6.
  • Trajectory of the growth rate (trajectory, through enw_simulate(growth_rate)): growth, decline, flat, rise_fall_sharp, rise_fall_gradual, fall_rise, oscillating, multi_wave.
  • Parametric delay family (delay_family, through enw_reference(distribution)): lognormal, gamma, exponential.
  • Delay form (delay_form, through enw_reference(parametric, non_parametric)): parametric, non_parametric, both.
  • Delay over time (delay_change, through enw_reference()): static, drift, step, periodic, walk.
  • Report days (reporting, through enw_report(structural)): daily, weekdays.
  • Day-of-week effect on expected counts (reference_dow, through enw_expectation(observation)): no, yes.
  • Observation model (observation, through enw_obs(family)): negbin, negbin1d, poisson.
  • Strata (strata, through enw_preprocess_data(by)): single, places, ages, places_ages.
  • How cases spread over strata (spread, through stratum_seed()): even, concentrated.
  • How places’ growth paths relate (place_rt, through place_growth()): shared, independent, correlated, lagged.
  • When places start (seeding, through late_strata()): start, mid.
  • How places infect each other (mixing, through place_mixing()): none, symmetric, asymmetric, distance, time_varying.
  • Strength of mixing between places (mixing_strength, through place_mixing()): weak, strong.
  • Imported cases (imports, through stratum_imports()): none, constant, pulses.
  • Reporting shocks in one or two places (shocks, through shock_window()): none, backlog, outage.
  • How places’ delays relate (delay_places, through delay_parameters()): shared, shifted, independent.
  • Observation noise across strata (obs_strata, through observe()): shared, dispersion, families.
  • Linked or independent strata (strata_link, through simulate_independent()): linked, independent.
Model

Expected onsets come from enw_expectation(), driven by a daily growth rate given to enw_simulate() as growth_rate. In a renewal process the growth rate is turned into log \(R_t\) under the generation time, and expected latent cases follow \(\lambda_t = R_t \sum_s g_s \lambda_{t-s}\). The seed is scaled so the expected outbreak size is a drawn target. The reporting delay is set by enw_reference(), as a parametric delay, a non-parametric hazard over delays, or both, and it may change over time. Report date effects and report days are set by enw_report(). Counts by onset day and delay are negative binomial (NB2 or NB1) or Poisson, set by enw_obs(), with the NB1 dispersion matched to the NB2 scale (epinowcast issue 937). The longest delay is the shortest of 3, 4, 6, 8 and 12 weeks at or above the delay’s 99th centile. A draw that fails or is out of size is drawn again in its cell, up to five times, after which the cell is skipped.

The grid has two limitations. Simulated strata are less sparse than BDBV’s provinces: the share of stratum-weeks with no cases reaches (not yet available) at the 95% point of the simulations, against (not yet available) for BDBV. Simulated delays are shorter than the German data’s, whose 99% point is (not yet available) days against (not yet available) at the 95% point of the simulations.

The simulator works around four limits of epinowcast’s simulation mode, each until the linked epinowcast change. It uses enw_simulate() from a feature branch of epinowcast (commit e084b080), until epinowcast#841 is merged. It reads the posterior predictive counts (pp_obs) from the fit directly, as enw_pp_summary() is slow, and runs each batch of 25 simulations under a 16 GB memory cap (epinowcast#841). Gamma reporting delays have a coefficient of variation of 0.8 to 1.6, as a shape above 2 gives NaN tail hazards (epinowcast#939). When the delay walks over weeks, its log spread walks at half the scale of its log location, as a zero scale sits on the parameter’s lower bound, where Stan cannot start (epinowcast#941).

The held-out synthetic set

The held-out set is simulated on grid r4 with seeds 30140 to 30143, 25 simulations each. We evaluate draw 1 of each simulation, so the outbreaks are independent. The fine-tune never trains on these seeds. The simulations are written to /home/runner/bdbv-rounds/rounds/r1/sims, and the workflow config records them. The synthetic runner runs the models on the set.

The held-out set was reserved for the final evaluation. No model was scored on it during development. We checked the pipeline on the r3 pilot seeds 9501 to 9504, whose forecasts are not results.

Protocol

Each outbreak is released weekly, on one weekday from Wednesday to Saturday drawn from its id, and a release’s cut-off is its origin. Each outbreak has four origins, spread evenly from its 6th release to the last release at least its longest delay plus 5 weeks before its last report. Each forecast covers weeks -4 to 3 from the week of the cut-off, as weekly totals by onset week for the country and each stratum. Scores use rolling truth, the count reported D days after the end of a target week, with D the outbreak’s own 98% reporting delay in whole days, and are relative to randomwalknowcast.

We cut the held-out simulations into blocks as in training, with the five layouts, report batching and non-summing parents (Table 2), using a fixed seed. A manifest records each held-out block’s layout and options. The paper’s test set, 100 simulations outside training, is cut the same way.

Correlated trajectories

The copula pairs the draws of models that give each place and week only its own draws. These are the Chronos-2 models and randomwalknowcast. Hub teams’ samples are joint across weeks within a place, so their pairing across places is left as submitted.

The copula. With \(P\) places and steps on days \(d_1, \dots, d_S\), each trajectory draws \(z \sim \mathrm{N}(0, \Sigma)\) with \(\Sigma = \Sigma_p \otimes \Sigma_t\). The week correlation is \((\Sigma_t)_{jk} = \rho_t^{|d_j - d_k| / 7}\). The place correlation is \(\rho_p\) between two provinces and \(\rho_n\) between the country and a province. Where \(\rho_p\) and \(\rho_n\) give no valid correlation matrix, we set its negative eigenvalues to zero and rescale it to unit diagonal. For each place-week, the \(m\) draws are sorted and the \(k\)th trajectory takes the draw whose rank matches the rank of \(z_k\) in that cell. Each cell keeps exactly its own draws (Clark et al. 2004; Schefzik, Thorarinsdottir, and Gneiting 2013). A country forecast as its own series, as by Chronos-2, is paired as one more place. A country that is the sum of its provinces is not paired but summed again from the paired provinces, so its forecast widens.

The estimator. For a forecast with \(m\) draws, \(b\) of them below the truth and \(e\) equal to it, the randomised PIT is \(u = (b + U(e + 1)) / (m + 1)\) with \(U \sim \mathrm{U}(0, 1)\). Its normal score is \(\Phi^{-1}(u)\). A forecast whose draws are all equal gives no score. Truth is the count as reported \(D\) days after the week, so a week’s error matures only once that release exists. At an origin, we use forecasts made at earlier origins whose truth was released by the origin, from the 8 latest such weeks. \(\rho_t\) is estimated from pairs of consecutive weeks of one place in one forecast. \(\rho_p\) is estimated from pairs of provinces in the same week of one forecast, and \(\rho_n\) from the country and a province. With \(n\) pairs whose Pearson correlation is \(r\), the estimate is \((n r + w \rho_0) / (n + w)\), with \(w = 100\) and \(\rho_0\) the prior. It is then kept within \([0, 0.95]\). Where the pairs give no correlation, the estimate is the prior. The prior \((\rho_t, \rho_p, \rho_n) = (0.9, 0.7, 0.7)\) is set by the energy score of the Chronos-2 models on the Ebola release backtest, so it is tuned in sample. As a sensitivity analysis we give the prior less weight, \(w = 25\).

Scores. We score the trajectories with the energy score (Gneiting et al. 2008) and the variogram score of order \(p = 0.5\) (Scheuerer and Hamill 2015), both on \(\log(x + 1)\) counts. We also report the 50% and 90% coverage of each place’s sum over weeks and of the sum over provinces in each week. WIS of each place and week is unchanged by pairing, except for a country summed from its provinces. We compare three versions of each model. Unpaired forecasts keep the sample indices the model gave. Fixed forecasts are paired with the prior at every origin. Rolling forecasts are paired with the copula estimated at each origin.

Aggregation options

Chronos-2 can forecast every level of a place hierarchy in one block, or each level on its own. We compare four options for the zero-shot and fine-tuned models, daily and weekly: (a) all levels in one joint block; (b) each level in its own block; (c) the lowest level only, summed up to the others with draws paired at random; (d) the lowest level only, summed up after pairing its draws with the rolling copula. Option (d) tests whether pairing the draws with the copula before summing narrows the gap to the joint block.

Impact of correlated trajectories

Pairing changes no marginal forecast, so it shows in the joint scores and in the coverage of sums over weeks and over places. Table 7 and Table 8 compare unpaired forecasts with the fixed and rolling copula for every model that uses it. The rolling estimates have median \(\rho_t\) 0.54 and \(\rho_p\) 4.0e-2 by German state, and \(\rho_t\) 0.43 and \(\rho_p\) 0.18 by age group, for randomwalknowcast.

Joint block or aggregation

Our Chronos-2 models use the joint block. On BDBV it is best or tied at provinces, zones and the national level for the zero-shot models. On the German development half, level by level is competitive for the daily zero-shot model (2.19 against 3.05 at the national level) and for age groups. Summing the lowest level loses at every level above it, with or without the copula: at BDBV’s national level the daily zero-shot model scores 8.01 summed and 5.11 summed after pairing, against 1.26 for the joint block. All WIS are relative to randomwalknowcast on the same targets. Table 9 and Table 10 give every model and level, and Table 11 what each option costs.
